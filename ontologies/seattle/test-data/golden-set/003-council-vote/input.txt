Seattle City Council Approves Affordable Housing Initiative in 7-2 Vote

SEATTLE - In a decisive vote Wednesday afternoon, the Seattle City Council approved a landmark affordable housing initiative aimed at creating 5,000 new units over the next five years.

The measure passed 7-2, with Council President Sara Nelson leading the effort alongside Council Members Teresa Mosqueda, Andrew Lewis, Tammy Morales, Dan Strauss, Cathy Moore, and Joy Hollingsworth voting in favor. Council Members Bob Kettle and Tanya Woo voted against the proposal.

"This is a transformative step for Seattle's housing future," Council President Nelson said following the vote. "We are committing to real solutions for our residents who need affordable places to live."

The Affordable Housing Initiative, sponsored by Council Member Mosqueda, allocates $600 million over five years for new construction and rehabilitation of affordable units. The funding will come from a combination of city reserves and projected tax revenue.

Council Member Kettle, who opposed the measure, expressed concerns about the fiscal impact. "While I support affordable housing goals, I believe the funding mechanism needs further refinement," Kettle said.

The vote took place during the regular City Council meeting at Seattle City Hall. Mayor Tim Burgess issued a statement supporting the initiative and pledging executive branch cooperation in implementation.

Key provisions of the initiative include:
- Priority for projects serving households at 60% area median income or below
- Requirement for accessible units in all new developments
- Expedited permitting for qualifying projects
- Partnerships with local housing nonprofits

The initiative takes effect January 1, 2026.
